## 1. Confirmed ownership and custody model

The user-confirmed policy is controlling for stock transferred to a distributor:

| Dimension | Rule for stock held by a distributor |
|---|---|
| **Inventory owner** | Aama Clothings owns the inventory. Transfer to a distributor does not transfer title or create a wholesale sale. |
| **Physical custodian** | The distributor holds and handles the stock in its authorized distribution role. Custody is not ownership. |
| **Physical location** | The distributor outlet/location, transit location, or return/quarantine location must be represented separately from the owner. |
| **Condition / availability** | Sellable good units, reserved units, in-transit units, damaged/quarantined units, missing/disputed units, and approved write-off are distinct states. A receipt declaration or aggregate count does not make stock saleable by itself. |
| **Quantity / variant** | Track whole units by canonical product-size-color SKU and location. Record the source transaction and each state change. |
| **Financial responsibility** | This policy does not assign loss/damage/shortage liability to a distributor. No receivable, penalty, expense, or write-off should be automatic. Apply only an independently approved, evidenced resolution under a separate policy. |
| **Supporting evidence** | Link production or accepted procurement source, approved transfer, dispatch/shipment, distributor receipt/checklist, actor, time, discrepancy and inspection evidence, and any later sale/return/correction. Portal declarations or carrier status are not independent physical proof. |

Consequences:

1. Dispatch is a location/custody transfer, not a sale.
2. Distributor receipt confirms reported custody and condition, not ownership.
3. Unsold distributor-held goods remain Aama inventory.
4. Customer sale is the relevant commercial event for sale-related revenue and inventory relief, subject to the approved recognition and costing policies. Order creation, assignment, reservation, or delivery status alone is not company cash.
5. Distributor customer collection is distinct from company Treasury receipt. Remittance, internal Treasury posting, and independent bank reconciliation are separate states.
6. Unsold stock returned from a distributor to a company location remains Aama inventory; that custody/location movement is not a sales reversal.
7. Damage, loss, shortage, and disputed counts require evidence and review. Do not automatically create distributor receivables or expense postings.
8. Distributor-held stock is neither distributor-owned inventory nor manufacturer consignment. Do not create distributor AP/AR merely because stock was transferred.

This confirmation resolves ownership **while stock is held by a distributor**. It does not by itself resolve title and recognition for manufacturer-held stock before dispatch, supplier-acquired stock before acceptance, loss-risk allocation, tax treatment, or the inventory costing method. Those remain separate decisions.

## 3. Contradictions resolved and remaining open

### Resolved by the confirmed policy

- Distributor-held inventory is Aama-owned; distributor receipt and custody do not transfer ownership.
- Stock transfer is not a sale, and stock receipt does not create distributor AP/AR or a second manufacturer product payable.
- The prior wording in the target-accounting document that manufacturer-owned consignment continues “until sale/delivery” must not be applied to stock already transferred to a distributor. It is narrowed for this state by the confirmed P5-R14 distributor-ownership policy.
- The older manufacturer/distributor architecture plan and Admin operations guide already describe platform-owned distributor stock and custody-only stock returns; P5-R14 makes that rule explicit and governing.

### Still open; do not silently resolve

- **Manufacturer-held stock before distributor dispatch:** current production code records platform ownership and recognizes manufacturer payable at completion; the older `04-target-accounting-domain-model.md` describes manufacturer consignment and payable/COGS at delivery. The distributor rule does not decide the pre-dispatch legal title, risk, or production accounting policy.
- **Supplier-purchased stock:** no active accepted supplier receipt/cost layer exists; title and recognition require an approved procurement policy.
- **Loss/damage/shortage responsibility:** custody status and discrepancy evidence do not establish contractual liability. No distributor receivable, penalty, expense or write-off should be posted automatically.
- **Customer return ownership/disposition:** company ownership of distributor-held stock does not define whether returned goods are restockable, repairable, quarantined, written off, or refunded; accepted receipt, inspection, source cost and approved policy are still needed.
- **Costing and accounting:** selected FIFO allocations, production layers and source-scoped account 1140 checks do not establish one complete inventory costing method, total 1140 valuation, or 5100 COGS completeness.
- **Physical and external evidence:** source code, user-uploaded images, OTP and internal Treasury records are not independent physical count, title contract, or bank confirmation.

The P5-R13 report's prior recommendation to make a new ownership choice for distributor-held stock is superseded: that specific policy is now confirmed. Its separate upstream manufacturer recognition question and single-writer inventory cutover remain open.

### Movement invariants

1. Transfer conservation by SKU and source line: dispatched quantity equals accepted good + damaged/quarantined + missing/disputed + still-in-transit quantities, with partial receipts and later corrections represented explicitly. No quantity disappears or becomes sellable solely from carrier status.
2. Distributor `InventoryLocation` and `InventoryLedgerEntry` owner is `PLATFORM` for this policy; no transfer/receipt event changes it to a distributor owner.
3. `InventoryBalance` is updated atomically with the matching movement on every supported write path; reservations are reconciled separately.
4. Each movement has a stable business source key, actor, event time, reason/evidence appropriate to its type, and idempotent retry behavior.
5. Cost-layer quantities are conserved across receipt, transfer, fulfillment, return, damage/loss and correction; missing layer linkage is an exception, never a catalog-cost fallback.
6. Internal custody changes have no sale/AP/AR effect. Customer sale and actual cash receipt/remittance are distinct source events.
7. A shortage or damage event changes operational status only as supported; financial responsibility and expense/receivable recognition require a separate evidenced and approved decision.
8. Reconciliation never auto-corrects balances. A correction is append-only, linked, authorized, and tested against downstream movements and accounting allocations.
